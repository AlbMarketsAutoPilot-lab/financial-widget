Kris Ann's Journey (KAJ) is a free geography board game and trivia quiz in one, for 2 to 4 players. You travel the world, answer quiz questions about flags, capitals and currencies, and make smart choices to collect the most points before your turns run out.

There are no dice: you choose every move. Luck adds a little spice, but good planning wins. This guide explains every rule, the four travellers, and our best tips.

The rules in 30 seconds

  • 2 to 4 players: people or robots.
  • Each player chooses a traveller and a starting continent.
  • One turn = one action: walk to a neighbouring country, or travel faster.
  • New countries, new continents and wonders give points.
  • Planes, ferries, trains, buses and cars cost points, or are free if you pass a quiz.
  • Buy businesses and citizenship, and the other players pay you.
  • When the last turn ends, the most points wins.

Getting ready

  • Players: 2 to 4 on one phone or tablet. Each seat is a person or a robot (at least one person). Robots play at easy, normal or hard level.
  • Game length: 30 turns (53 areas, 7 wonders) or 50 turns (84 areas, 11 wonders, a bigger and more detailed world).
  • Choose your traveller: in turn order, each player picks one of the four travellers. Each traveller can be taken by one player only.
  • Choose where to start: each player picks a different starting continent (Europe, Asia, Africa, North America, South America or Oceania), then a starting country.

Big countries are all or nothing: you get their points only when every part is visited. You can leave halfway and come back later to finish. Points can never go below 0.

Moving around the world

On your turn you do one thing. Most of the time you walk to a neighbouring country, which takes one turn and costs nothing. Two players can never stand in the same area, and a place someone is flying or sailing to is booked until they arrive.

To go faster, take a plane ✈️, ferry ⛴️, train 🚆, bus 🚌 or car 🚗. Airports and ports have one to three fixed destinations, and you choose one when you board.

Pay with points, or with knowledge

A ticket costs points. But planes, ferries, trains and buses also offer an airline quiz: answer one question about your destination correctly and you travel for free. A wrong answer loses that turn. After three wrong answers in the same place you must pay, if you can. If you can't afford the ticket, you can keep trying the quiz on each turn until you get it right. You have 15 seconds to answer.

In Mexico you can rent a car (no quiz) and drive across all of the USA at once. The Backpacker can't rent a car: it tries to hitchhike instead 👍.

Challenges on the way

On long trips another passenger may challenge you to a quiz. You can say yes or no. Say yes, and you win +1 point if you're right or lose 1 point if you're wrong. There are six kinds of challenge:

  • Which flag? A flag is shown: pick the country.
  • Which is bigger? Pick the bigger of two countries.
  • Which capital? "Capital of Peru?" Lima or Quito.
  • Which continent? "Where is Mali?" Africa or Asia.
  • Neighbours: "Which one borders France?" Spain or Poland.
  • Which currency? "Japan pays with…" Yen or Won.

The four travellers

Each traveller plays differently, so every game feels new.

  • 🎒 Backpacker: +7 points for reaching 3 continents. Never pays for tickets: always takes the free quiz. Can take the bus and try to hitchhike. But: slow trips.
  • 💼 Business Traveler: +6 points when granted citizenship. Fast trips, and can rent a car in Mexico. But: tickets cost 2 points, and no bus.
  • 💎 Luxury Traveler: flies or sails to any airport or port, and gets citizenship with no test. Can rent a car in Mexico. But: tickets cost 3 points, and no train or bus.
  • 💻 Digital Nomad: +1 point for every trip (it works on the way) and +5 for reaching 3 continents. The cheapest tickets; can take the bus and rent a car. But: never citizenship, no travel challenges, and slow trips.

Citizenship and visas

Once per game you can ask for citizenship of the country you are standing in (not your home country). On your next turn you take a short test: read 6 facts about the country, then answer 3 questions. Get 2 or more right and you become a citizen. Fewer, and your one request of the game is used up. The Luxury Traveler gets citizenship at once, with no test.

From then on, every other player pays you a 2-point visa each time they enter that country. No points, no visa, no entry: a player who can't pay must find another way, for example an airport or port and a free quiz ticket.

Breaking news

Every few turns your phone buzzes with news from the country you are in. Some news brings points, some takes them away. It happens to everyone!

Winning

When the last turn ends, the player with the most points wins. Businesses count for their price. If two players are tied, the one who visited more continents wins, and if they are still tied, the one who visited more countries. Your game is saved automatically, so you can stop and continue later.

Tips to win

  1. Plan your route before you walk. Turns are limited, and going back to a country you already visited gives nothing.
  2. New continents are worth it. +2 for each new continent often pays for the ticket.
  3. Finish big countries. Half a country gives 0; all of Canada gives +5.
  4. Know your geography? Use the quiz. A correct answer is a free ticket, and you keep your points.
  5. Buy where people pass. An airline or ferry on a busy route earns again and again.
  6. Choose citizenship wisely. Pick a country the others must cross: every visit pays you 2 points.
  7. Play to your traveller. The Backpacker should aim for 3 continents early, the Business Traveler for citizenship, the Luxury Traveler for long jumps across the map, and the Digital Nomad for many trips.
  8. Young players? Start with 30 turns and robots on easy.

What kids learn

Kris Ann's Journey is a fun way to learn world geography together: flags, capitals, currencies, continents, neighbouring countries and the size of countries, plus hundreds of fun facts about countries, wonders, food, animals, languages and inventions. Kids also practise planning ahead, managing points and making decisions.

Questions

Is Kris Ann's Journey free?
Yes. No price, no ads, no in-app purchases, no accounts, no chat and no personal data collected.

Can I play alone?
Yes. Play against 1 to 3 robots, on easy, normal or hard.

Does it need the internet?
No. The apps work fully offline.

Where can I play?
In your web browser at kaj.kritere.com, on Windows PC from the Microsoft Store, or on Android from Google Play.
