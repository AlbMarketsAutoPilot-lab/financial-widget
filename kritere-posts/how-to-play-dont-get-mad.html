Don't Get Mad! is a free family dice board game for 2 to 4 players. You roll the die and race your button from your front door to the Festival of Lights, and every square on the way is a little travel story: hot-air balloons, wrong ferries, sheep with right of way, and keys you forgot (twice).

If your family loves classic race games like Ludo or Snakes and Ladders, you will feel at home: you need a 6 to start, you can bump other players back to the start, and some squares fly you forward while others send you back. On top of that come lanterns, Bonus cards and a few surprises that keep everyone in the game until the very last square.

Don't Get Mad! started on paper: a father and his daughter drew the whole board by hand, square by square. This guide explains every rule, then gives our best tips.

The rules in 30 seconds

  • 2 to 4 players: people, the computer, or both.
  • Roll a 6 to leave Home. Then roll again and move.
  • Every 6 gives you another roll. The rolls add up (up to three 6s).
  • Green ▲ squares help you, red ▼ squares set you back.
  • Land on another player and they go back to the start.
  • Lanterns and Bonus cards can save you from bad luck.
  • Reach the last square with the exact number to win.

The goal

Be the first player to reach the final square, the Festival of Lights. Nobody is safe and nobody is lost: the leader can fall near the finish, and the player in last place can still fly forward and win. That's the whole fun, and why the game is called Don't Get Mad!

Getting ready

  • Players: 2 to 4 on one phone, tablet or computer. Any seat can be a person or the computer.
  • Computer players: choose Easy or Normal. Normal players use their lanterns cleverly.
  • Choose your journey: the Quick Trip (100 squares) for a short break, or the Grand Journey (202 squares, the original hand-drawn board) for a long evening.
  • Each player gets a button token in their own colour and 3 lanterns.

The first time you open the game, a short "How to play" guide shows you the basics in eight pages. You can open it again at any time from the start screen.

Rolling a 6 to start

Everyone begins at Home, off the board. To start, you need to roll a 6. The 6 lets you out but doesn't move you: you roll again and move by that roll (6, then 2: you go to square 2).

No 6 after three tries? You start anyway and lose one spare lantern (if you only have your last lantern, you simply keep trying until you roll a 6). In a hurry? You can also spend a spare lantern to leave Home at once, without waiting for a 6.

Sixes add up

Every time you roll a 6, you roll again, up to three times. Then you move once by the total: 6 + 6 + 3 = 15 squares in one move. Three 6s in a row move you 18 squares, end your turn and earn you an extra lantern.

Lanterns: your lifelines

You start with 3 lanterns. They are what you carry to the Festival of Lights, and they can save you:

  • Leave Home or the Bog at once by spending a spare lantern.
  • Shield yourself: when you land on a red ▼ square, spend a spare lantern to cancel it.

Your last lantern is never lost: you need it to light up at the finish, so nobody is ever knocked out of the game. A shield protects only you, and it doesn't work against a bump, the fallen tree, or the square where the other players double their next roll.

Good and bad squares

About every fifth square is an event. Green ▲ squares help you, red ▼ squares set you back. Squares you pass over do nothing: only the square where you stop counts. You can tap any square on the board to read what it does before you roll.

Some of the things that can happen:

  • Big jumps forward: a hot-air balloon, a cable car or a hay cart carries you far ahead.
  • Falls back: a booking that never existed, a wrong ferry, or forgetting your keys sends you back.
  • Swap with the leader: change places with the player closest to the finish.
  • Others double: the other players double their next roll.
  • Roll two or three times and move by the total.
  • Next good square: jump to the nearest green square ahead.

A square reached because of an event doesn't trigger again, unless the square says so.

The Bog

The Bog is the trap of the journey. Land there (or on a square that sends you there) and you are stuck until you roll a 6. Like at Home, that 6 gets you out, then you roll again and move. If you hold the Bog boots you can walk straight out, and a spare lantern also gets you out at once.

Bonus card and Bog boots

Two squares on the board give you a Bonus card. You can use it once, whenever you like, in one of two ways:

  • cancel your own punishment; or
  • spoil another player's reward: when someone lands on a big jump, the game asks if you want to cancel it. "Their lucky break? Cancel it."

The Bog boots let you walk out of the Bog at once.

Bump! Only one player per square

If you land on a square where another player stands, by a roll or by any jump, they go back to the start. There are no safe squares. The good news for them: they don't need another 6, they just move on their next turn.

The fallen tree

At the start of each round a fallen tree may appear a few squares in front of the leader. The first player to land on it goes back 10 squares, then the tree is cleared. Passing over it does nothing.

The Snake Leader

At the seaside, a sea snake may give you a lift far ahead and make you the Snake Leader. But be careful: if the Snake Leader lands on the very last square before the finish, the snake wants its ride back and sends them down the board.

Winning: the exact number

You must reach the final square with the exact number. If you are 1 square away, you need a 1. Roll more and you stay where you are. The first player to arrive is the Champion, and everyone else gets a rematch with one tap.

Along the way you collect postcards from the journey, 16 in total, kept in your postcard album.

Tips to win (and to stay calm)

  1. Keep a spare lantern for the Bog. Waiting for a 6 there can take many turns.
  2. Save your shield for big falls. Don't spend a lantern on a one-square step back.
  3. Use the Bonus card on the leader's big jump. Spoiling a hot-air balloon near the end can change the whole game.
  4. Watch who is behind you. A player 1 to 6 squares behind can bump you back to the start.
  5. Never give up. Swaps, jumps and the Snake Leader mean the last player can still win.
  6. Young children? Start with the Quick Trip and the computer on Easy.

Questions

Is Don't Get Mad! free?
Yes. No price, no ads, no in-app purchases, no accounts and no data collected.

Can I play alone?
Yes. Play against 1 to 3 computer players, on Easy or Normal.

How long is a game?
The Quick Trip (100 squares) is a short game for a break; the Grand Journey (202 squares) is much longer, perfect for a long evening.

Where can I play?
In your web browser at dgm.kritere.com, on Windows PC from the Microsoft Store, or on Android from Google Play. The apps work fully offline.

Is it like Ludo?
It shares the best parts of Ludo and Snakes and Ladders (a 6 to start, bumping players, big jumps up and down) with one token per player and a story on every square.
